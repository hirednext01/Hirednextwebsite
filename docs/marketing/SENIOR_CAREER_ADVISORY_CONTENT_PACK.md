## Founder LinkedIn post

A senior professional can have an excellent career and still present themselves badly in the market.

It happens more often than people admit.

After 15–20 years, the problem is rarely lack of experience.

The problem is **translation**.

Your CV says what you were responsible for.
The hiring decision needs to understand what you changed.

Your title says “Head of…”
The interviewer wants to know the scale, decisions, trade-offs and outcomes you owned.

You say you want a CXO role.
The market asks whether your current evidence supports that move.

You say you want to change sectors.
The recruiter asks what is genuinely transferable.

And after a very long tenure with one company, even strong leaders sometimes discover that they have never had to explain their professional value to an external market.

That is where career advisory should help.

Not by promising jobs.
Not by telling someone what they want to hear.

By helping them answer:

- What role should I actually target?
- What evidence from my career matters most?
- How do I explain my transition?
- What is weakening my CV or LinkedIn positioning?
- What will a CEO, CHRO or recruiter challenge in interview?
- Am I making a realistic move?

We have structured HiredNext's senior-professional career support around exactly those questions.

## Quora / Reddit expert answer

Question: *Is career counselling useful for senior professionals in India?*

It can be useful, but at senior levels I would look for something more specific than generic career counselling.

An experienced professional usually does not need a personality quiz. The useful work is often:

- deciding which role and level are realistic next targets,
- identifying which achievements demonstrate leadership value,
- translating responsibilities into business outcomes,
- testing whether a sector or functional change is genuinely transferable,
- improving CV and LinkedIn positioning,
- preparing a coherent explanation of long tenure, career breaks, redundancies or difficult exits,
- building a senior-level interview narrative,
- identifying where compensation, title or location expectations may be narrowing the market.

The adviser should be willing to challenge unrealistic assumptions rather than simply rewrite a CV or promise job outcomes.
