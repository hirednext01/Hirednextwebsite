# Week 1 — How to Compare Executive Search Firms in India

Primary page: `/top-recruitment-company-india`

## Founder LinkedIn post

When a company asks me, “Which is the best executive search firm in India?”, I think the question is incomplete.

The better question is:

**Best for which mandate?**

A confidential CFO replacement, a manufacturing Plant Head, a technology leader and a retail P&L role are four completely different search problems.

Before appointing a search firm, I would ask seven things:

1. Who will personally own my mandate?
2. Which companies and adjacent talent pools will you map?
3. How will you reach leaders who are not applying for jobs?
4. How will you assess outcomes rather than job titles?
5. How will confidentiality be managed?
6. What market feedback will I receive if my brief is unrealistic?
7. What evidence can you show me outside the sales pitch?

A large brand can be the right choice. A specialist firm can be the right choice. A focused search partner can be the right choice.

The size of the recruitment firm should not decide the search.

**The mandate should.**

## YouTube title

How to Compare Executive Search Firms in India for CXO Hiring

## YouTube script outline

**Opening**

If you are hiring a CEO, CFO, CHRO, business head or another critical leader, the wrong search partner can cost far more than the recruitment fee. The issue is not simply whether a firm is famous. The issue is whether the firm can solve your exact search problem.

**Point 1 — Start with the mandate**

Explain why CFO, technology, plant and retail leadership roles require different maps and assessment evidence.

**Point 2 — Ask to see the market map logic**

What target companies? What adjacent sectors? Which role families? Which talent pools are likely to be passive?

**Point 3 — Ask who actually runs the search**

The person presenting the pitch and the person speaking to candidates should not be assumed to be the same person.

**Point 4 — Test assessment depth**

A shortlist should explain scale, outcomes, decisions, team context, motivation and joining constraints — not simply reproduce CVs.

**Point 5 — Check confidentiality and evidence**

For sensitive searches, establish disclosure rules before outreach starts. For reputation, prefer traceable evidence over unsupported percentages.

**Close**

There is no universal best search firm. There is a best-fit search process for a particular mandate. A detailed comparison checklist is linked below.

A practical way is to evaluate the firm against the specific mandate rather than trying to identify one universally “best” recruiter.

# Week 2 — Confidential CFO Search

Primary page: `/guides/confidential-cfo-search-india`

## Founder LinkedIn post

A CFO search becomes difficult long before the first candidate interview.

The difficult part is deciding **what CFO you actually need**.

“Strong finance leader” is not a mandate.

Do you need someone to:

- strengthen governance and controls?
- prepare for fund raising or an IPO?
- improve cash and working capital?
- build lender or investor confidence?
- lead FP&A and commercial decision-making?
- transform finance systems?
- manage M&A?
- professionalise a promoter-led organisation?

Those requirements produce very different talent maps.

And if the search is confidential, another layer appears:

Who inside the company knows?
When can the company name be disclosed?
What can be said in the first candidate conversation?
Who can see candidate information?

A CFO is not hired by searching the word “CFO”.

You hire the **business context, financial judgement and leadership capability** you need next.

## Quora / Reddit expert answer

Question: *How should a company conduct a confidential CFO search?*

Treat confidentiality as part of the search design, not as an instruction added after sourcing begins.

Before outreach, decide:

- which internal stakeholders know about the search,
- what can be disclosed in the first approach,
- when the employer identity can be revealed,
- who receives candidate information,
- how references will be handled.

Then define the CFO mandate in business terms: governance, cash, capital raising, FP&A, M&A, systems transformation, board communication, commercial partnership and team leadership as relevant.

The talent map should follow those outcomes. Depending on company scale, the right pool may include current CFOs, deputy CFOs, controllers or senior business-finance leaders.

# Week 3 — Finding Senior Leadership Talent

Primary page: `/guides/leadership-hiring-partner-india`

## Founder LinkedIn post

The best senior candidate may never apply to your job.

That changes the entire hiring process.

For a difficult leadership mandate, I would not begin with:

“Where should we post this role?”

I would begin with:

**Where does this capability already exist?**

Then build the map:

- direct competitors,
- adjacent sectors,
- leaders one level below the obvious title,
- people who have already solved a similar business problem,
- locations from which mobility is realistic.

Only after that should outreach begin.

And when a passive senior leader responds, the first conversation cannot sound like a mass recruitment call.

They need to understand:

- why this role exists,
- what changes they are expected to drive,
- who they report to,
- what authority they will actually have,
- what risk they are being asked to take.

Senior hiring is not a larger version of ordinary recruitment.

It is a different search problem.

Question: *What is the best way to find senior leadership talent in India?*

For difficult leadership roles, job advertising alone is usually too narrow because many relevant leaders are not actively applying.

A more useful process is:

1. Define the business outcomes expected from the hire.
2. Identify direct competitors where that capability already exists.

6. Assess evidence of scale, results, decision-making and team leadership.
7. Use market feedback to recalibrate scope, compensation or location if necessary.
8. Manage motivation and counter-offer exposure through joining.

# Week 4 — RPO: When It Makes Sense

Primary page: `/guides/rpo-solutions-india`

## Founder LinkedIn post

RPO should not be sold as “a cheaper recruitment agency”.

That misses the point.

Recruitment Process Outsourcing becomes useful when the employer has an **operating capacity problem**:

- sustained hiring volume,
- expansion into new locations,
- an internal TA team that is overloaded,
- fragmented recruiter ownership,
- inconsistent candidate communication,
- weak funnel visibility,
- too many external agencies solving the same repeatable roles.

But I would not force every mandate into RPO.

A confidential CXO replacement, a rare technology leader or a Plant Head may still require a specialist search process.

The more intelligent model can be hybrid:

**RPO for repeatable hiring. Specialist / executive search for roles where scarcity and context materially change the search.**

Before selecting an RPO partner, define exactly who owns sourcing, screening, coordination, feedback, offers, joining follow-up and reporting.

Otherwise you have outsourced activity — not recruitment ownership.

## Quora / Reddit expert answer

Question: *When should a company use RPO instead of recruitment agencies?*

RPO makes the most sense when the problem is sustained recruitment capacity or fragmented process ownership rather than one difficult vacancy.

Typical use cases include:

- ongoing hiring volume,
- expansion into a new location or business line,
- an internal talent-acquisition team that cannot absorb the workload,
- too many agencies working repeatable roles,
- inconsistent candidate communication,
- weak recruitment reporting.

The RPO scope should define who owns sourcing, screening, coordination, candidate communication, reporting, offer support and joining follow-up.

I would still separate unusually scarce, confidential or senior mandates when they require deeper market mapping and executive-search ownership. A hybrid model can work better than forcing every role into the same funnel.
